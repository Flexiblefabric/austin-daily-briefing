## Research and source eligibility

Search Austin, Travis County and directly relevant Central Texas sources across civic affairs, housing, transportation, development, public safety, courts, health, environment, business and community life. Deliberately check consequential public-risk and accountability reporting before closing the pool. Include a separate search across enabled interests for MFY.

Search Under the Radar independently through official agendas, public records, community organizations and specialist reporting even if the headline slate is full. Record the overlooked fact and specific evidence that readers could reasonably miss it. Narrow scope alone does not prove underreporting. A failed headline candidate is not automatically an Under the Radar discovery.

Read accessible credible evidence supporting every central claim. Prefer primary records and original reporting. Source confidence is an eligibility gate and never earns scoring points. Replace inaccessible sources or omit the item. Verify the promised resource experience; a video title or an interactive landing page alone is insufficient when the claimed value depends on playback or operation. A readable transcript may be selected as a transcript on its own merits.

State whether an action is proposed, pending, adopted, completed or alleged. An agenda cannot establish a completed vote. Preserve uncertainty and attribution. Record publication/update time and generation cutoff in America/Chicago. Unknown timestamps remain unknown. In subsequent comparisons, distinguish pre-generation omissions from developments arriving between generation and send, and from post-send developments.

## History, material change and freshness

Use at least 14 days of actual published production history, by normalized key and topic similarity. Resolve delivery status using the existing queue/history records; queued or failed content is not proof the reader received it. Preserve operational idempotency independently: do not recreate existing message IDs.

Shared coverage history concerns shared Top Stories and Under the Radar; personalized history concerns MFY for the applicable profile. A profile's prior MFY item must not suppress consequential shared news for everyone. Shadow selections are not actual reader history at cutover. If actual history is unavailable, report the retrieval failure instead of assuming a clean history.

For previously covered topics:
1. Identify the prior date and actual facts already conveyed.
2. Record Material Update TRUE or FALSE and the exact verified change.
3. Reject unchanged repeats before scoring.
4. Score eligible material changes on incremental value.
5. Optionally apply Freshness Veto after scoring when a real change still offers insufficient additional reader value.

A new vote, ruling, funding decision, lawsuit, construction phase, cancellation, actual opening/closure, meaningful new data, hearing outcome or substantively changed deadline may qualify. The calendar advancing to an already announced hearing, event or deadline does not.

Record event timing separately as upcoming/today/completed. A useful reminder can qualify for Austin Ahead under its own repeat-utility rules. It must not enter ranked news solely because it happens today.

Freshness Veto may demote or omit a scored material repeat in any applicable section. Preserve its score and TRUE material-update label. Log a short reader-value reason. Ordinary rank displacement is not a veto. Freshness Veto does not promote anything and does not become a permanent preference or penalty.

For evergreen resources, track normalized topic/resource identity and last actual inclusion in existing published Briefing History. Extend lookup beyond 14 days for known resource repeats; an expired lookup window or refreshed URL is not new value. Retain the existing data retention policy; do not invent permanent subscriber records. When resource history cannot be established, do not claim first exposure and hold an ambiguous repeat rather than infer novelty. Reuse requires a substantive new capability or information, not a fresh date alone.

## Shared Top Stories and Under the Radar

### Shared 100-point score

Use whole-number component scores. Do not substitute, merge, add, remove, or reweight factors.

| Component | Maximum | Scoring question |
|---|---:|---|
| Local impact | 20 | How broadly or deeply could this affect people in the Austin area? |
| Consequence | 20 | How significant, durable, costly, risky, or institutionally important is the development? |
| New-information value | 15 | For a new topic, how much genuinely new value does it provide? For a repeat, how meaningful is the material change and its incremental reader value? |
| Austin relevance | 15 | How directly is the story tied to Austin residents, institutions, decisions, geography, or daily life? |
| Timeliness / actionability | 10 | Why does it matter now, and can readers prepare, respond, attend, participate, or make a near-term decision? |
| Public-interest value | 10 | Does it improve civic accountability or understanding of safety, equity, access, public systems, or community conditions? |
| Underreported / discovery value | 10 | Does ADB add value by surfacing a consequential development readers could reasonably miss? |
| **Total** | **100** | |

### Shared scoring anchors

For each component:

- `0`: absent or effectively irrelevant
- Approximately half credit: meaningful but limited, indirect, or mixed
- Full credit: unusually strong and clearly demonstrated

Explain component scores using the candidate's facts. Do not assign totals impressionistically. The base score must equal the sum of all seven displayed components.

A candidate normally needs a base score of at least **60** to remain eligible. No editorial control may rescue a candidate below 60.



The shared score is independent of profile preferences. Off interests do not suppress shared public-interest coverage.

Choose about five Top Stories, fewer if necessary rather than filler. A consequential independently discovered item goes to Top Stories when broad prominence is warranted; do not also publish it Under the Radar. Under the Radar must meet the same shared 60-point floor, with a documented overlooked fact and placement rationale. It may be empty.

Apply shared Selection Balance after scoring. Preserve scores and identify affected candidates and reasons. Normally avoid more than two Top Stories from the same meeting, press release or substantially identical underlying development. This is not a subject quota. Distinct accountability investigations must not be omitted solely because other stories also concern public safety, justice or the same institution. Compare the particular facts and consequences. MFY's five-point variety rule does not replace shared Selection Balance.

Austin Pulse previews the chosen Top Stories and is not a separate scoring lane. Its overlap with those stories is intentional. Weather remains a utility section; an exceptional weather development can independently qualify as a Top Story.

## More for You

Personalization applies only here. Use actual saved High/Normal/Off preferences and the active Interest Catalog's scope. Local food and event items should satisfy their local scope; broadly scoped science or gaming content does not need an artificial Austin consequence. Do not invent genre preferences, family circumstances or interest settings.

Before scoring, require source/access eligibility, enabled-interest fit, repeat eligibility and correct section placement. Exclude Off topics. For adjacency, record saved interest → specific source feature → reader payoff. A shared word, brand, headline or broad category alone is insufficient. Do not reintroduce an Off topic through an adjacent label.

Avoid duplicating shared stories. A genuinely distinct personalized angle is a separate candidate only when it offers substantively different value, independently passes all gates, and explains why it is not a retelling.

| Component | Maximum | Calibration anchors |
|---|---:|---|
| Interest relevance (I) | 25 | 21–25: direct, substantive High match. 16–20: direct Normal match or unusually strong, explicit adjacency. 11–15: defensible but narrower adjacency. A title/category match without a content connection fails eligibility. Off fails eligibility. |
| Reader value (V) | 25 | 0–9: almost no payoff beyond awareness. 10–14: limited payoff. 15–19: clear worthwhile understanding, enjoyment, creation, participation, or decision value. 20–25: strong and specific payoff. |
| New-information value (N) | 20 | 0–4: little beyond prior ADB coverage. 5–9: small addition. 10–14: meaningful new-to-ADB explanation or capability. 15–20: substantial new information or capability. First exposure alone does not earn full credit. |
| Timeliness (T) | 10 | 0–2: weak current reason. 3–4: currently usable evergreen resource. 5–7: relevant near-term window. 8–10: strong current development or time-sensitive utility. |
| Substance (S) | 10 | 0–4: headline, teaser, promotional assertion, or bare listing. 5–7: enough concrete detail to deliver the promised value. 8–10: developed explanation, useful comparison, demonstrated method, or functional resource. |
| Discovery value (D) | 10 | 0–3: familiar or obvious. 4–6: useful curated finding. 7–10: distinctive, surprising, or plausibly overlooked. Old does not automatically mean obscure. |
| **Total** | **100** | **I + V + N + T + S + D** |

New-information value measures what the item adds to the applicable ADB coverage. Discovery value measures the distinctiveness of finding it. Do not award both merely because an item is unfamiliar. Enjoyment and creative inspiration count as reader value; public-policy consequence is not required.

Source confidence remains a gate, not bonus points. Shared Top Stories and Under the Radar continue using their existing shared rubric. MFY totals must never be compared numerically with shared-story totals.


The approved MFY floor is 60/100, with reader value at least 15/25 and substance at least 5/10. Require all three. These are editorial operating choices, not statistically optimized thresholds. Display component scores internally; never add civic locality points or score source confidence.

Reader value includes enjoyment, understanding, creative inspiration and participation. Evergreen resources are eligible when usable and worthwhile; publication age alone neither excludes them nor earns discovery credit. Avoid fabricated urgency, novelty or claims about the reader's personal knowledge.

Rank eligible items, then apply:
- Ordinary variety: a replacement may be at most 5 total-score points below the displaced candidate and at most 3 reader-value points lower. Compare content and purpose; no quotas.
- Discovery Promotion: at most one per profile/run, for a specific distinctive or surprising benefit. The replacement may be at most 10 total-score points below the displaced candidate and at most 3 reader-value points lower. Zero promotions is valid.
- Both controls preserve scores, sourcing, Off, repeat, section and component gates. Record the displaced item and rationale. Do not label the same move as both controls or chain swaps to evade a limit; measure displacement against the original score-ranked eligible slate.

Saved Fewer/Standard/More volume remains authoritative. Standard's roughly 3–5 useful items is a capacity guide, not a minimum. Shorter or empty is acceptable. Do not treat the three-slot calibration fixture as a new production setting.

Add no reader-facing labels or badges for evergreen, discovery, scoring or controls. Preserve existing section orientation and normal source attribution. Classifications and audit labels are internal.
