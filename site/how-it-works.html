How ADB Works

A short briefing with a deliberate process behind it.

Austin Daily Briefing is designed to help readers understand what matters in Austin, what changed, why it matters, and what is worth watching next—without turning staying informed into another task.

The briefing comes first.

Most editions begin with a set of roughly five Top Stories. These are not personalized. Every subscriber should receive the same core view of the day’s important Austin developments.

Weather, Austin Pulse, Under the Radar when warranted, and Austin Ahead provide additional orientation and utility around that core briefing.

Stories are discovered across more than one lane.

ADB looks across credible local reporting, official information, public records, community developments, and other useful sources. Discovery is broader than the final briefing: finding a story does not automatically make it important enough to publish.

Under the Radar uses a separate discovery idea. Its purpose is to surface consequential information readers could reasonably miss, not to recycle stories that failed to make the Top Stories list.

Selection is about consequence, not just recency.

Eligible stories are evaluated for what has materially changed, how much the development affects daily life or civic understanding and how timely it is. The final group gives readers a useful picture of the city rather than five versions of the same subject.

In development: A next-generation V2 story-selection engine is currently being tested through read-only shadow reviews. It does not yet determine the live production briefing. This page describes stable editorial principles rather than presenting V2 as a finished production system.

Why It Matters separates event from consequence.

The summary answers “What happened?” Why It Matters answers “What changes because of it?”

The distinction is intentional. A vote, announcement, filing, opening, closure, or report can be easy to summarize while its practical consequence is less obvious. Why It Matters gives that consequence its own space and uses cautious language when an outcome remains uncertain.

Under the Radar is not filler.

Under the Radar appears only when there is a consequential development that deserves attention and is plausibly being missed. It can be absent. A rejected Top Story does not automatically become an Under the Radar item.

Personalization is contained in More for You.

Subscribers can choose interests and reading preferences. Those choices shape More for You, which can include Austin topics as well as broader subjects a subscriber has selected.

Personalization does not alter Top Stories. The goal is to add relevance without creating a different civic reality for every reader.

Austin Ahead is for near-term usefulness.

Austin Ahead highlights things worth attending, watching, or planning around, generally within the coming week. Items use one practical label:

  • GO for something to attend, visit, experience, or participate in.
  • WATCH for an approaching development primarily worth being aware of.
  • PLAN for something that requires preparation, registration, rerouting, scheduling, or another practical adjustment.

Sources stay visible.

ADB depends on outside reporting and primary information. It is not currently an original-reporting newsroom. Substantive items should link readers to the publisher or original source so they can review the underlying material themselves.

Strong sourcing is a requirement for publication, not a substitute for editorial judgment.

Automation helps with the work. Responsibility stays human.

Automated and AI-assisted tools may help discover, compare, organize, summarize, personalize, validate, and deliver information. Human oversight sets the publication’s priorities, designs the rules, reviews performance, and remains responsible for the finished product.

ADB is an independent project. It is not affiliated with the City of Austin, another government agency, or the publications and sources it links to.

A new day is not automatically a new story.

ADB reviews recent briefing history to avoid repeating essentially the same development. A story can return when there is a material update: for example, a vote, ruling, funding decision, lawsuit, construction phase, hearing outcome, cancellation, actual opening or closure, or meaningful new data.

A calendar date advancing by itself is generally not a material change.

Corrections are part of the product.

When ADB confirms a factual error that warrants correction, the affected information is corrected or clarified and the public Corrections log records the change when appropriate.

Readers can submit possible errors, tips, or general feedback through Feedback & Corrections or reply to the briefing.
